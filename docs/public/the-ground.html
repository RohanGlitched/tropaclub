      The Ground and the Real Holders method

      A holder count is not a crowd. Stock tokens on Base report tens of thousands of holders, and most of those wallets hold cents. Tropa Club counts the real holders of every token and draws each token’s holders as a ground, one seat per wallet.

      The Ground#

      Each stock token gets a ground: a drawing of its holders as a football stadium, such as the NVIDIA ground. Each wallet that holds the token is one seat. Real fans (real holders) are lit in the token’s colours. Ghost seats are faint: under $10 (dust), airdrop-only (farmer) wallets and contracts. The ranking of all minted tokens by real holders is the stock rankings page (also called The Attendance).

      The picture makes one point with numbers behind it. At the Nov 21 2026 count, 17,685 wallets held NVIDIA’s token, and only 1,613 were real fans. The NVIDIA ground draws that as 17,685 seats with 1,613 lit.

      The method, step by step#

      1. Replay every transfer.The indexer reads all Transfer logs of every minted Coinbase stock token on Base, from the first mint on Aug 12 2026 (block 49,860,000), and replays them into balances. The first full run replayed 21.8 million transfers across 90 tokens. After the first run it continues from the last block it applied, staying about 150 blocks behind the chain head so that it never records a block that could still be reorganised.
      2. Check the totals.Replayed balances are compared with the total supply each token reports. In the Nov 21 run they matched within 0.6% for every token, with no negative balances.
      3. Price each balance.A balance is valued at the DEX price of the token’s deepest pool with at least $1,000 of liquidity. That price already contains the token’s split and dividend multiplier. If there is no such pool, the underlying stock’s last price times the multiplier is used and the token is marked priceSource: "reference". If neither exists the token is unpriced and every seat shows $0.
      4. Tell people from code.Each address is checked for contract code. Plain wallets, EIP-7702 delegated wallets and smart wallets (Coinbase Smart Wallet and Safe style proxies, the kind wallet apps create) count as people. Liquidity pools and every other contract are ghost seats.
      5. Set giveaways aside.Some contracts send small amounts to thousands of wallets. A wallet is airdrop-only (farmer in the feed) if every incoming transfer came from one of those distributors, with no DEX purchase, no router delivery and no mint of its own.
      6. Count the fans.A wallet that clears all the tests is a real fan. Attendance is real fans divided by holders.

      Thresholds and classification order#

      Every seat gets exactly one kind. The tests run in this order, and the first one that matches decides the kind.

      OrderKind in the feedRule
      1poolA labelled liquidity pool or venue address.
      2contractThe address has contract code and is not a smart wallet or a delegated wallet.
      3farmerThe airdrop-only (farmer) test below, whatever the balance.
      4realBalance worth $10 or more at the token’s current price.
      5dustEverything else: a person’s wallet holding under $10.

      In the feed’s per-token summary, ghosts.contracts adds pools and contracts together, and ghosts.farmerOnly and ghosts.dust are counted separately. The three ghost counts plus realFans always add up to holdersTotal.

      Contract detection#

      The indexer calls eth_getCode for every address that ever held a stock token (54,389 of them in the first run) and sorts the result into four kinds: a plain wallet, an EIP-7702 delegated wallet, a smart wallet, and a contract. Delegated and smart wallets count as people because most active buyers use them: wallet apps delegate ordinary accounts to a shared implementation, and Coinbase Smart Wallets are proxy contracts. Known liquidity pools are labelled separately before this check.

      The farmer-distributor heuristic#

      A distributor is a contract that fans out small token amounts to many wallets: giveaways, reward boxes, campaign payouts. We label one when it looks like this:

      • it sends to at least 193 distinct recipients outside of swaps;
      • its pass-through ratio is below 0.4, meaning it is not simply relaying what it receives, as an aggregator router does;
      • it sends out at least twice what it takes in;
      • its median transfer is under $15.

      Contracts with a pass-through ratio of 0.4 or more are treated as routers, which deliver purchases, and a delivery from a router counts as the wallet’s own buy. Two named gift-box contracts are added to the distributor list by hand. A wallet is airdrop-only when all of its incoming transfers across every token came from distributors. In the Nov 21 count 11,597 wallets met that test for at least one token.

      Why a heuristic

      A giveaway recipient is a real person, and some of them later buy for themselves. That is why the rule is about every incoming transfer: one purchase of their own makes a wallet a normal wallet, and it can become a real fan.

      Dust#

      Earlier research used $1 as the dust line (66% of all holders were under it). We use $10: a person’s wallet under $10 and not otherwise classified is dust. If you want the under-$1 slice, read each seat’s usd field.

      Ranking rules#

      Real fans are ranked. The seat on a ticket, the real-fan number and the “best stat” all come from this ranking.

      1. Unbroken hold time in days, longest first. A hold is unbroken while the balance stays above zero.
      2. Earliest first buy, earliest first, breaking ties between equal hold times.
      3. Last-resort ties are broken by when the current hold started and then by address, so the order is the same on every run.

      Size never counts

      How much a wallet holds never affects its rank or its seat. The top ten wallets own 72% to 90% of every token (35% to 76% once pools are removed), so ranking by dollars would show the same few names for ever. Rank one sits pitchside at halfway.

      A wallet’s best stat is the one where it sits highest among real fans of that token, shown as “top N%”. The candidates are earliest buyer, longest unbroken hold, entry price against the median real fan’s entry, held through a weekend reopen gap, and weekly buy streak.

      Buyer counts#

      A weekly buyer is a person’s wallet that ends up with a token from a DEX purchase in the last seven days. Wallets with more than 50 swap events across all stock tokens in that week are treated as bots and left out. A new buyer made their first DEX buy of that token inside the window. In the first full count (Nov 21 2026) the median weekly buy was about $5 to $10. Later counts publish the median for each week, so a figure on the site can differ.

      Update cadence#

      The indexer runs every six hours, at 17 minutes past 00:00, 06:00, 12:00 and 18:00 UTC. Each run publishes the files described in the data feed and stamps them with asOf (the time of the snapshot) and block (the last Base block applied). The files are cached for five minutes, so a page can be up to six hours and five minutes behind the chain.

      Known approximations#

      The count is public chain data run through rules we publish. It is accurate about what the chain shows and honest about what it cannot see.

      • A wallet is not a person. One person with several wallets is several seats, and a shared wallet is one. We do not cluster wallets by owner.
      • Prices are spot prices. A seat’s usd value is the balance at the current DEX price, not what the wallet paid. A wallet at $9.80 today is dust and may be a fan tomorrow. DEX prices trade around the clock and can sit a little off the underlying stock when the market is closed.
      • Unpriced tokens have no fans. A token with no liquid pool and no reference price has no dollar line. At the Nov 21 count, 38 of the 90 minted tokens were unpriced.
      • The airdrop-only (farmer) rule is a label, not a verdict. It is built from observed behaviour. A wallet that received only giveaways and never bought is set aside. A wallet that hides a purchase inside a transfer from a friend is counted as a normal wallet.
      • Hold time resets when a wallet empties or moves. Moving tokens to a fresh wallet starts a new hold in that wallet and ends the old one.
      • Entry price is estimated. Entry is the daily average DEX price on the day of each buy, weighted by size. Transfers in and giveaways have no entry price.
      • Counts lag by design. The six-hour cadence and the 150-block lag mean a purchase made a minute ago will not be visible yet.

      Check your wallet#

      Paste a wallet at tropaclub.xyz and the camera flies to its seat. A real fan gets a ticket for each token: the ground, block, row and seat from its rank, its best stat, and a barcode drawn from the address bytes. A ticket is a picture to share, not an NFT, and it never prints a dollar amount. Everything on it is also in the public data feed.
