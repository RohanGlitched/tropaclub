This page lists what Tropa Club will not do, where it will not let you buy, what its numbers cannot tell you, and the risks of the tokens themselves. Read it before you put money in.

What Tropa Club is not

  • Not investment advice. Nothing in the app or these docs tells you what to buy, sell or hold. No stock is picked, tipped or scored. The app does not know your situation.
  • Not a broker or a custodian. Tropa Club holds no money, no tokens and no keys. Every transaction is signed in your own wallet. No Tropa Club contract is in the path of your funds. See non-custodial, 0% fee.
  • Not an issuer. The tokens are issued by Coinbase’s onchain SPV. Tropa Club does not mint, redeem or guarantee them.
  • Not affiliated with the companies named. Club names such as “NVIDIA FC” are nicknames for a token’s holders. The companies have nothing to do with them.

No prizes tied to returns

No prize, rebate, fee reduction or access depends on how well anyone’s holdings did. Fixtures compare the median of members who played, and the points reward showing up. The brand does not use odds, bets, jackpots, arrows or win language around money, and every card and table that shows history says “past performance · not advice”. See the rule.

Region rule

The buy flow, club buttons and call-ups are hidden in the United States and its territories: US, PR, GU, VI, AS, MP and UM. Before you buy, the app also asks you to confirm that you are not a US person.
  • The country comes from the edge of the network that serves you, and it is used only for this check and for pick-up matching. It is never stored against your wallet.
  • Read-only pages stay open everywhere: The Ground, The Attendance, tickets, the data feed and these docs.
  • The rule is a minimum, not a legal opinion. Rules for tokenized securities differ by country and change, and we cannot list them all. You are responsible for knowing whether you may buy where you live. If you are not sure, do not buy.

Data limits

The numbers on The Ground and in the feed are counts of public chain data under published rules. They are a good description of what the chain shows. They are not a census of people. The full list is in known approximations. In short:

  • A wallet is not a person, and we do not link wallets to owners.
  • Values are spot DEX prices at the time of a snapshot, six hours old at worst, not what anyone paid.
  • Tokens with no liquid pool are unpriced and have no real fans.
  • The farmer rule and the bot rule are heuristics. They can mislabel a wallet.
  • Returns on Matchday cards are time-weighted percentages on Tropa Club buys only. They are a status, not a performance record, and they are not tax or accounting advice.
  • The cost-basis and P&L statement is labelled “FIFO · USD · records, not tax advice”. It adjusts for splits and stock dividends. Tokens that were transferred in show “basis unknown”.
  • Retention figures are descriptive, not causal. See Retention evidence.

Risks of stock tokens

A stock token follows a share price, and it can lose value. It also has risks a share in a brokerage account does not.

Weekend and after-hours gaps

Stock tokens trade on DEXs every hour of every day. The company’s shares trade on weekdays during market hours. Price feeds for the tokens hold the last close on weekends and holidays. In between, the DEX price is the market’s own guess and can sit above or below the frozen reference. On Sat Nov 21 2026 the gaps ranged from about 2% above the frozen NAV (MSTRc +1.98%, ANTc +1.83%) to about 1% below it (SNDKc −0.96%). When the market reopens, the price can jump to meet the stock: across the weekends we measured, the reopen gap ran from −5.4% to +2.6%. This is why the buy guard holds a buy when the pool has drifted too far from the last close.

Corporate actions

Splits and stock dividends are recorded through a multiplier on the token (ERC-8056). The number of tokens in your wallet does not change when the multiplier does. The number of shares it represents does. Twelve tokens had a multiplier above 1 on Nov 21 2026, and SNDKc stood at 8. During a corporate action the reference price freezes, and Tropa Club pauses buying that token until the action is over. A change in the multiplier with no announcement is possible. In that case the statement labels it “unclassified” and shows it both ways.

Issuer pauses and policy blocks

The issuer can pause transfers of a token, and compliance policies on the tokens can block individual addresses, for example sanctioned ones. If either applies, your swap fails or buying is held. Selling can be affected too. Tropa Club cannot override either.

Other risks

  • Thin pools. Most trading is on a few pools, and many small tokens have little liquidity. A guard limits a fill to 1.5% off the fair price. It cannot create liquidity.
  • Issuer and custody risk. The token depends on the issuer and the custodian holding the underlying shares.
  • Smart contract risk. The token, the router and the pool are code. Code can fail.
  • Private-company tokens. Some tokens track companies that are not listed. Their price comes from the pool alone and can sit far from any funding-round valuation.
  • No investor protection to assume. A stock token is not a brokerage account. Do not assume the protection schemes that apply to accounts apply to it.
  • You can lose all of it. Only buy with money you can afford to lose.

Privacy

  • Reading is anonymous. You do not sign in to look at The Ground, a ticket or these docs. Looking up a wallet is not stored against that wallet.
  • No IP address is stored against a wallet. Country comes from a network header, is used for the region check and for matching, and is not saved with your address. The pick-up queue keeps only a salted hash of it and deletes the entry when you are seated.
  • What a club stores. The wallet addresses of members, an optional nickname, lineups, and the Tropa Club buys made through the app: the transaction hash, token, amount and time. All of this is already public on the chain except the club membership.
  • One extra lookup. To keep two wallets with the same funder out of the same pick-up club, we record which address first funded a wallet.
  • The exclusion list is public. Founder and test wallets are listed and removed from counts.
  • Everything on the feed is public chain data. It contains wallet addresses and nothing else about a person.
